GreenTrack AI

In Benin's green spaces, plants are watered on a fixed schedule, whether it has rained or not. GreenTrack AI predicts, plant by plant, when the soil will run dry, and waters just in time and just enough — or not at all if rain is coming.

Member 2 — 40 s Today, there are three ways to water a green space. 1. On a fixed schedule: we water even after rain, and every plant gets the same dose, whatever its species. 2. The worker's rounds: they take hours, never cover the whole park, and it's judged by eye. 3. The sensor with a fixed threshold — that was our first version: it waters when the soil is already dry. It reacts too late, and it doesn't know it's going to rain in three hours. What all three lack: anticipation. Knowing in advance when each plant will be thirsty. Transition: “That's exactly what our AI does.”

Member 2 — 1 min GreenTrack AI comes down to three steps. 1. At the foot of each plant, a small self-powered unit: a soil moisture probe at 20 cm, an ESP32, a solar panel and a battery. It takes a reading every 15 minutes. 2. A model predicts soil moisture for the next 24 hours. It uses the plant's history, the weather forecast — heat, rain — and the species: a neem, a baobab or a coconut palm don't dry out at the same rate. 3. We water just before the plant drops below its threshold, with the smallest useful dose. And if the forecast rain is enough, we don't water. Show the chart: in white, what was measured; in green, what the AI predicts. The neem will drop below its threshold in about 15 hours: the AI schedules 18 litres in 12 hours. Transition (hand over): “Our teammate will show you where it gets hard.”

Member 3 — 50 s Three challenges, and this is where the real AI work is. 1. Sensors drop out: at the back of a park, the signal is weak. On the prototype, 9 sensors out of 116 are offline. Rather than leave a gap, we estimate moisture from neighbouring plants, and we flag outlier readings — a broken sensor must not trigger watering. 2. A new plant has no history. We start from a model shared by its species, which we fine-tune to that specific plant over the weeks. 3. The weather is uncertain. We don't bet on rain: we wait only if the plant can hold out even if it doesn't rain. Transition: “Here's where our prototype stands.”
